# Introduction to the Pew Research Center Survey on Astrology, Tarot Cards, and Fortune-Telling

In a world increasingly shaped by data and rational inquiry, the enduring appeal of astrology, tarot cards, and fortune-telling remains a compelling cultural phenomenon. To better understand the role these practices play in the lives of Americans, the Pew Research Center conducted a nationally representative survey of 9,593 U.S. adults in October 2024. The survey, part of the American Trends Panel, aimed to assess how many Americans engage in these practices, how frequently they do so, and what demographic factors influence their beliefs and behaviors. The study was weighted to ensure the sample accurately reflects the U.S. adult population across key demographics such as age, gender, race, ethnicity, political ideology, and income level [citation:txt1].

The interest in New Age and esoteric practices has grown substantially in recent years, particularly among younger generations. This surge is often attributed to increased media attention, the rise of social media platforms, and a cultural shift toward spirituality and personal exploration [citation:txt3]. During the pandemic, many individuals turned to these practices as a source of comfort, guidance, and connection in uncertain times. The Pew Research Center’s findings offer a timely and data-driven look at the prevalence of astrology, tarot cards, and fortune-telling in the United States and their place in the broader cultural landscape.

The survey’s timing—fall 2024—coincides with a period of heightened public interest in metaphysical and spiritual practices, especially among young adults and those identifying as part of the LGBTQ+ community. These groups have shown a disproportionate engagement with such practices, as seen in preliminary data from the survey [citation:txt9]. Moreover, the rise of astrology and tarot content on platforms like Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube has likely contributed to the normalization of these traditions, making them more accessible and socially acceptable across a wide range of demographics.

By examining the survey results, we can better understand how Americans are incorporating these ancient practices into their modern lives. Do they see them as tools for decision-making, or simply as entertainment and self-reflection? What role do gender, age, and political orientation play in shaping these beliefs?
