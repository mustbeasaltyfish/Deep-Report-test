# Introduction to the Pew Research Center Survey on Astrology, Tarot Cards, and Fortune-Telling

In a world increasingly shaped by science and technology, many Americans continue to turn to age-old practices such as astrology, tarot cards, and fortune-telling for guidance and entertainment. To better understand the prevalence and cultural significance of these practices, the Pew Research Center conducted a nationally representative survey of 9,593 U.S. adults in October 2024. The survey, part of the American Trends Panel, aimed to assess how often these practices are used, which demographic groups are most engaged, and whether they are viewed as a form of fun or as a tool for major life decisions [citation:txt1].

The survey results reveal that 30% of U.S. adults consult astrology, tarot cards, or a fortune teller at least once a year [citation:txt3]. While this number may seem surprising in a scientifically literate society, it reflects a broader cultural shift toward New Age and spiritual practices, particularly among younger generations. This trend has been amplified by the rise of social media, where astrology and tarot content often goes viral and attracts millions of followers. The pandemic also played a role in this resurgence, as many individuals turned to these practices during periods of uncertainty and isolation [citation:txt14].

The survey was carefully weighted to ensure that the sample accurately represented the U.S. adult population across key demographic factors such as age, gender, race, and income. This methodological rigor allows for reliable insights into the patterns of belief and engagement with these practices. As the following sections will explore in greater depth, the data reveals significant differences in how various groups interact with astrology, tarot cards, and fortune-telling, offering a nuanced picture of their role in contemporary American life.
