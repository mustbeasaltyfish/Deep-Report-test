# Introduction to the Pew Research Center Survey on Astrology, Tarot Cards, and Fortune-Telling

In a world increasingly shaped by data and science, the enduring appeal of astrology, tarot cards, and fortune-telling practices remains a compelling cultural phenomenon. To better understand the extent of their prevalence and the ways in which Americans engage with them, the Pew Research Center conducted a nationally representative survey of 9,593 U.S. adults from October 21 to 27, 2024. This survey, part of the Center’s American Trends Panel (ATP), aimed to shed light on the frequency and context of these practices in American life, particularly in light of growing media attention and the influence of social media platforms, especially among younger demographics [citation:txt1].

The study is weighted to ensure that the results accurately reflect the broader U.S. adult population by key demographic factors such as gender, age, race, ethnicity, and income. The findings are particularly timely, as recent years have seen a resurgence of interest in New Age practices, fueled in part by the cultural shifts of the COVID-19 pandemic and the rise of digital platforms where such practices are readily accessible and widely shared [citation:txt3].

This survey reveals that 30% of U.S. adults consult astrology (or a horoscope), tarot cards, or a fortune teller at least once a year. However, the majority of these consultations are not taken as serious guidance for major life decisions; instead, they are often engaged with for entertainment or personal curiosity [citation:txt5]. This distinction is crucial, as it suggests a nuanced relationship between Americans and these practices—viewed more as tools for reflection or leisure rather than as authoritative sources of life direction.

The cultural landscape surrounding these practices has evolved significantly in recent years. The rise of astrology influencers on platforms like Instagram and TikTok, the proliferation of tarot card apps, and the increased visibility of New Age spirituality in popular media have all contributed to a broader normalization of these practices, particularly among Gen Z and younger millennials. These trends have sparked both fascination and skepticism, making the Pew Research Center’s findings an important contribution to understanding how Americans navigate the intersection of tradition, spirituality, and modernity [citation:txt7].

As the first section of this article, the focus here is on establishing the scope, methodology, and context of the survey. The following sections will explore the demographic patterns in engagement with astrology, tarot cards, and fortune-telling, as well as the motivations behind these practices. The data collected in this study not only provides a snapshot of current trends but also invites deeper inquiry into the evolving role of spirituality and personal guidance in American society.
