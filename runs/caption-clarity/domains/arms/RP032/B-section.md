# Introduction to the Pew Research Center Survey on Astrology, Tarot Cards, and Fortune-Telling

In a world increasingly shaped by science and technology, many Americans continue to turn to age-old practices such as astrology, tarot cards, and fortune-telling for guidance and entertainment. A recent survey conducted by the Pew Research Center sheds light on the prevalence of these New Age practices in the United States and explores the factors that influence engagement with them. The study, carried out in October 2024, involved 9,593 U.S. adults and was conducted using the American Trends Panel (ATP), a nationally representative online survey panel. The survey was weighted to reflect the U.S. adult population across key demographic categories, ensuring a balanced and accurate representation of American views and behaviors [citation:txt1].

The research comes at a time when New Age practices are receiving heightened attention, particularly on social media platforms and in mainstream media. This increased visibility has coincided with a broader cultural shift toward spirituality and self-reflection, especially among younger generations. The pandemic, in particular, has been linked to a surge in interest in personal growth, mental well-being, and alternative forms of guidance—trends that have only accelerated in recent years [citation:txt1].

The survey results reveal that 30% of U.S. adults consult astrology, tarot cards, or a fortune teller at least once a year, offering a snapshot of how these practices are embedded in the American cultural landscape [citation:txt3]. As the data suggests, these practices are not confined to a niche audience but are instead part of the lived experiences of a significant portion of the population. The next sections of this article will delve into the frequency of these practices, the demographic patterns that define their users, and the motivations behind their continued popularity.
