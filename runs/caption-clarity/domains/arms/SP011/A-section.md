# Defining Blue-Collar Workers and Survey Methodology

Blue-collar workers have long played a vital role in the U.S. economy, contributing to the production and maintenance of goods and infrastructure through manual and physical labor. Traditionally associated with industries such as manufacturing, construction, and transportation, the term "blue-collar" reflects both the physical nature of the work and the historical work attire of these laborers, who often wore durable blue uniforms to withstand the demands of their jobs [citation:txt8]. In modern economic contexts, blue-collar work is distinguished from white-collar work, which typically involves office-based, intellectual, or managerial tasks [citation:txt13].

For the purposes of this analysis, the Pew Research Center defines blue-collar workers as individuals who perform manual or physical labor in one of five specific industrial sectors: manufacturing, mining and construction; agriculture, forestry, fishing, and hunting; retail and trade; hospitality or service; and transportation [citation:txt1]. These sectors collectively account for 39% of the U.S. workforce, with those whose jobs involve manual or physical labor—classified as blue-collar workers—comprising 27% of the labor force [citation:txt14]. This definition ensures a consistent and measurable framework for analyzing the experiences and perceptions of blue-collar workers in comparison to other types of workers.

To explore how blue-collar workers perceive their jobs, the Pew Research Center conducted a nationwide survey in October 2024. The survey included 5,273 employed U.S. adults, of whom 1,004 were classified as blue-collar workers based on the criteria outlined above [citation:txt4]. The survey was conducted between October 7 and October 13, 2024, and aimed to capture a representative sample of the U.S. workforce across various demographic and geographic categories [citation:txt9]. The sample included individuals working part-time or full-time, with a primary job, and was weighted to ensure demographic accuracy [citation:txt19]. This rigorous methodology provides a reliable foundation for understanding the broader experiences of blue-collar workers in the U.S. labor market.

By defining blue-collar workers through both job function and industry classification and by employing a robust survey methodology, this study aims to provide a nuanced understanding of job satisfaction, career perceptions, and work-related challenges among this significant segment of the workforce.
