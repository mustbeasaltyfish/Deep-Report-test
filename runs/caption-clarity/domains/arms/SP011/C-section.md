# Defining Blue-Collar Workers and Survey Methodology

Blue-collar workers have long played a vital role in the U.S. economy, contributing to the production and maintenance of goods and infrastructure through manual and physical labor. Traditionally associated with industries such as manufacturing, construction, and transportation, the term "blue-collar" reflects both the physical nature of the work and the historical work attire of these laborers, who often wore durable blue uniforms to withstand the demands of their jobs [citation:txt8]. In modern economic discourse, blue-collar work is distinguished from white-collar work, which typically involves office-based, intellectual, or managerial tasks [citation:txt13].

For the purposes of this analysis, the Pew Research Center defines blue-collar workers as individuals who perform manual or physical labor in one of five specific industrial sectors: manufacturing, mining and construction; agriculture, forestry, fishing, and hunting; retail and trade; hospitality or service; and transportation [citation:txt1]. These sectors collectively account for 39% of the U.S. workforce, with those whose jobs involve manual or physical labor—classified as blue-collar workers—comprising 27% of the labor force [citation:txt14]. This definition ensures a consistent and sector-based classification that reflects the realities of today's labor market, where the boundaries between manual and non-manual work have become increasingly blurred [citation:txt15].

To better understand the experiences and perceptions of blue-collar workers, the Pew Research Center conducted a survey of 5,273 employed U.S. adults between October 7 and October 13, 2024 [citation:txt4]. Of this sample, 1,004 individuals were classified as blue-collar workers based on the criteria outlined above. The survey aimed to capture a broad and representative sample of the U.S. workforce, including individuals across different age groups, genders, racial and ethnic backgrounds, and political affiliations [citation:txt9]. The sample was weighted to ensure accurate representation of the general population, and detailed demographic breakdowns are available for further analysis [citation:img4].

This survey provides a valuable snapshot of how blue-collar workers perceive their jobs in comparison to other workers, particularly in terms of job satisfaction, career prospects, and work conditions. By focusing on these key areas, the study aims to shed light on the unique challenges and opportunities faced by blue-collar workers in the current labor market.
