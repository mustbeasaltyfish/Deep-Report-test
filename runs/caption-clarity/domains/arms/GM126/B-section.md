# The Longest Peaceful Border and Its Maritime Disputes

The U.S.-Canada border is widely recognized as the longest peaceful and undefended land boundary in the world, stretching approximately 5,525 miles (8,891 km) across land and water. Despite its reputation for tranquility and cooperation, this vast boundary conceals a set of complex and unresolved maritime and territorial disputes that could, under different political or economic circumstances, become flashpoints of contention. While the two nations have historically maintained a strong and cooperative relationship, the existence of these disputes reveals the fragility of international boundaries, even between the closest of neighbors [citation:txt1].

Though the land border is largely settled and peaceful, the maritime boundaries between the United States and Canada remain a source of disagreement in several key areas. Specifically, there are five ongoing maritime and territorial disputes between the two countries. These disputes span both the Arctic and Pacific Oceans and include disagreements over the delimitation of exclusive economic zones (EEZs), continental shelf claims, and the status of specific islands and straits [citation:txt3]. While the land border has been meticulously mapped and negotiated over the course of centuries, the maritime boundaries remain unresolved due to historical ambiguities, conflicting legal interpretations, and the evolving nature of international maritime law [citation:txt4].

These disputes are not merely academic or historical in nature; they carry significant practical implications. The areas in contention often overlap with regions rich in natural resources, including fisheries and potential hydrocarbon reserves. For example, the Beaufort Sea, a focal point of one of the most contentious disputes, is believed to hold substantial oil and gas reserves. The unresolved boundary in this region has led to competing claims over resource rights, with both nations interpreting historical treaties—particularly the 1825 Convention between Great Britain and Russia—differently [citation:txt10].

The existence of these disputes highlights the fact that even the most cooperative of international relationships can be strained by unresolved territorial issues. While the U.S. and Canada have demonstrated a willingness to engage in dialogue—such as the 2024 announcement of a joint task force to negotiate the Beaufort Sea boundary—progress has often been slow and incremental [citation:txt15].
