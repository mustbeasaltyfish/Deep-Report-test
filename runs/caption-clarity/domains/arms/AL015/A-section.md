# Introduction to Learning with Limited Labeled Data

In many real-world applications of machine learning, the availability of labeled data is often limited due to the high cost or difficulty of obtaining expert annotations. Supervised learning, which relies heavily on labeled examples, can suffer from poor generalization when the training set is small. This challenge has led to the development of several strategies to improve model performance in low-data regimes. Among these, four primary approaches have emerged as particularly effective: pre-training plus fine-tuning, semi-supervised learning, active learning, and pre-training with dataset auto-generation.

The first approach, **pre-training plus fine-tuning**, leverages large-scale pre-trained models that have learned rich representations from vast amounts of unlabeled data. These models are then fine-tuned on the small labeled dataset specific to the target task. This strategy has been especially popular in domains like natural language processing, where pre-trained models such as BERT and Llama have demonstrated impressive performance even when fine-tuned on limited labeled samples [citation:txt16].

The second approach, **semi-supervised learning**, combines both labeled and unlabeled data during training to improve model accuracy. By incorporating unlabeled data, these methods aim to capture the underlying data distribution and leverage the structure present in the input space. Techniques such as pseudo-labeling, consistency regularization, and deep generative models have been widely explored in this context [citation:txt1].

**Active learning** is the third strategy, where the model itself participates in the annotation process by selecting the most informative samples for labeling. This approach aims to reduce the overall labeling effort by focusing on data points that provide the most value for training. Active learning is particularly useful in settings where annotation costs are high, such as in medical imaging [citation:txt11].

The fourth approach, **pre-training plus dataset auto-generation**, builds upon the success of pre-trained models by using them to generate additional labeled data. In this method, a pre-trained model is used to label unlabeled samples, effectively creating a larger synthetic training set. This technique has proven effective in domains like natural language processing, where few-shot learning and large language models enable the generation of high-quality pseudo-labeled data [citation:txt7].
