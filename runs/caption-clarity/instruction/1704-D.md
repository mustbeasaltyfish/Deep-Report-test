# The Challenge of Privacy-Preserving Fine-Tuning in Large Language Models

Fine-tuning large language models (LLMs) on domain-specific data is a powerful approach to adapt these models to specialized tasks, such as healthcare, finance, or legal analysis. However, such data is often highly sensitive, containing personal or confidential information, which raises significant privacy concerns [citation:txt15]. The risk of data leakage—where the model inadvertently memorizes or reveals private information from the training data—has become a critical barrier to the widespread deployment of LLMs in privacy-sensitive applications [citation:txt19].

To address this challenge, differential privacy (DP) has emerged as a rigorous mathematical framework for ensuring privacy during model training [citation:txt11]. By introducing carefully calibrated noise into the training process, DP guarantees that the output model is statistically indistinguishable whether or not any individual's data is included in the training set. This provides a strong, quantifiable privacy guarantee, making DP an essential tool in privacy-preserving machine learning [citation:txt20].

The most common form of DP applied in deep learning is *example-level differential privacy*, where each individual training example is protected. Techniques such as DP-SGD (Differentially Private Stochastic Gradient Descent) achieve this by clipping per-example gradients and adding noise proportional to the privacy budget $\varepsilon$ [citation:txt1]. While effective in certain settings, example-level DP has notable limitations in real-world scenarios. For example, when a single user contributes multiple training examples—common in applications like federated learning—example-level DP may not sufficiently protect the user’s overall data contribution [citation:txt7]. An attacker could potentially infer the presence of a user in the training set by analyzing the patterns across their multiple examples, undermining the intended privacy guarantees [citation:txt6].

To overcome this, *user-level differential privacy* offers a stronger and more realistic privacy guarantee. In user-level DP, the privacy of an entire user's contribution is protected, meaning that the model's output should remain nearly identical regardless of whether all the data from a single user is included or excluded [citation:txt16]. This is particularly important in modern data ecosystems, where data ownership is increasingly concentrated at the user level, and users may contribute large numbers of examples [citation:txt8]. For instance, in federated learning, where training occurs across distributed user devices, each user’s data is naturally grouped, making user-level DP both necessary and well-suited to the setting [citation:txt7].

The adoption of user-level DP in datacenter-based training environments—where large LLMs are typically fine-tuned—presents a unique opportunity. Unlike federated learning, datacenter training allows for greater flexibility in algorithm design and optimization, enabling the exploration of novel techniques for user-level DP [citation:txt9]. This flexibility opens the door to more efficient and effective DP mechanisms, which is the central focus of this article: how can user-level DP be scaled to large language models in datacenter settings, and what algorithmic optimizations make this possible?
