# The Challenge of Fine-Tuning LLMs on Sensitive Data with Privacy Guarantees

Fine-tuning large language models (LLMs) on domain-specific data is often essential for achieving high performance in real-world applications, such as healthcare, finance, and personal assistants. However, this process raises significant privacy concerns, as the training data typically includes sensitive user information. The challenge lies in adapting these models to specialized tasks while ensuring that individual or user-level data cannot be inferred or reconstructed from the model's outputs. This is where differential privacy (DP) comes into play, offering a mathematically rigorous framework to quantify and limit privacy loss during model training [citation:txt11].

Differential privacy achieves this by introducing carefully calibrated noise into the training process, ensuring that the model's output distribution does not significantly change based on the inclusion or exclusion of any single data point or user's data. In the context of LLM fine-tuning, this means that the model should remain insensitive to the presence of any individual user’s contributions, even if they provided multiple examples. This is crucial for preventing privacy violations such as membership inference attacks, where an adversary could determine whether a particular user's data was used in training [citation:txt6].

Traditionally, example-level DP has been the standard approach, where each individual training example is protected. This is typically implemented using techniques like DP-SGD, which clips per-example gradients and adds noise calibrated to the privacy budget $\varepsilon$ [citation:txt1]. However, this approach has notable limitations when users contribute multiple examples to the training set. For instance, a user with many data points may still be identifiable, as the cumulative effect of their contributions can be inferred from the model [citation:txt5]. In such cases, example-level DP may not provide sufficient privacy guarantees, especially in scenarios where data points from the same user are highly correlated [citation:txt15].

To address this, user-level differential privacy (DP) has emerged as a stronger and more realistic privacy notion. User-level DP ensures that the model's output remains insensitive to the inclusion or exclusion of an entire user's data, regardless of how many examples they contributed. This is particularly important in modern data settings, where users may contribute large volumes of data, and privacy breaches could have severe consequences [citation:txt8]. For example, in federated learning, where training is performed across distributed devices, user-level DP is a natural fit because each device aggregates a user’s data into a single model update, making it easier to apply DP at the user level [citation:txt7].

While federated learning has been a primary setting for user-level DP, datacenter-based training also offers unique advantages for implementing and scaling user-level DP. In datacenter environments, training is centralized, allowing for more flexible algorithmic optimizations and efficient noise calibration. This flexibility can lead to better performance and tighter privacy guarantees compared to federated settings, where communication and computation constraints often limit the scope of DP mechanisms [citation:txt9].
