# The Challenge of Fine-Tuning Large Language Models with Privacy

Fine-tuning large language models (LLMs) on domain-specific data is often essential to adapt these models to specialized tasks, such as legal document analysis, healthcare diagnostics, or financial forecasting. However, this process introduces significant privacy risks when the training data contains sensitive information about individual users. In such scenarios, the models risk memorizing private details, which could be exploited through attacks like membership inference or model inversion [citation:txt15]. To address these concerns, differential privacy (DP) has emerged as a rigorous framework for quantifying and controlling privacy loss during model training [citation:txt11].

DP ensures that the output of a model training process remains stable even if the training data is modified by the inclusion or exclusion of a single data point. This is achieved by introducing calibrated noise into the training procedure, thereby making it difficult for an adversary to infer whether a specific individual contributed to the training set [citation:txt14]. While DP has been widely applied to machine learning, the granularity of the privacy guarantee is critical. Example-level DP, the most common form, ensures that the model's output is insensitive to any single training example. However, this approach can be insufficient when users contribute multiple examples, as it does not prevent the inference of a user's participation if enough of their data is present [citation:txt6].

This limitation becomes particularly acute in real-world applications where users may provide extensive and correlated data. For instance, in federated learning settings, users often contribute multiple examples from the same device or account, and an attacker could infer a user's presence in the training set by analyzing patterns across these examples [citation:txt7]. To address this, user-level differential privacy (DP) has been proposed as a stronger privacy guarantee. User-level DP ensures that the inclusion or exclusion of an entire user’s data has a negligible impact on the model’s output, regardless of the number of examples they contribute [citation:txt5]. This makes it exponentially harder for an adversary to determine if a user participated in the training process [citation:txt11].

While user-level DP is a more stringent requirement, it is particularly relevant in today’s data landscape, where users increasingly expect control over their data and the models trained on it. In datacenter settings, where centralized training is feasible, user-level DP offers greater flexibility compared to federated learning, which is constrained by communication and computational limitations [citation:txt9]. This flexibility allows for the exploration of algorithmic optimizations that can scale user-level DP to large language models, enabling both strong privacy guarantees and effective model performance.

The challenge, then, is to leverage this flexibility to develop practical and scalable algorithms for fine-tuning LLMs with user-level DP. This article investigates how different approaches—such as example-level sampling and user-level sampling—can be optimized to achieve this goal while maintaining model utility.
