# The Challenge of Fine-Tuning Large Language Models with Privacy

Fine-tuning large language models (LLMs) on domain-specific data is often essential to achieving high performance in specialized applications such as healthcare, finance, and legal domains. However, this process frequently involves sensitive user data, which raises significant privacy concerns. The need to protect this data is paramount, as models trained on such information can inadvertently memorize and leak private details about individuals or organizations [citation:txt15]. Traditional approaches to privacy, such as data anonymization, are increasingly inadequate in the face of sophisticated reconstruction attacks and model inversion techniques [citation:txt19].

Differential privacy (DP) offers a robust framework for training models with formal privacy guarantees. By introducing calibrated noise during the training process, DP ensures that the output of a model is insensitive to the inclusion or exclusion of any single data point [citation:txt11]. This approach has been widely applied to machine learning, particularly in the form of DP-SGD (Differentially Private Stochastic Gradient Descent), where gradient clipping and noise addition are used to limit the influence of any one example [citation:txt20].

However, in many practical scenarios, users contribute multiple examples to the training dataset—often with high correlations in language use, vocabulary, and context. In such cases, example-level DP, which protects the privacy of individual examples, may fail to adequately safeguard the privacy of the user as a whole. An attacker could potentially infer that a particular user contributed to the training data by analyzing patterns across multiple examples [citation:txt6]. This shortcoming highlights the need for stronger privacy guarantees that account for a user’s entire contribution to the training set.

User-level differential privacy (DP) addresses this limitation by ensuring that the inclusion or exclusion of all examples from any one user has a negligible impact on the model’s output [citation:txt5]. This stronger form of privacy is particularly relevant in federated learning and modern data ownership contexts, where users expect their entire data contribution to be protected [citation:txt8]. User-level DP is especially important in settings where users contribute large numbers of examples, as the privacy risk can grow exponentially with the number of examples per user [citation:txt16].

While federated learning has traditionally been the primary setting for implementing user-level DP, datacenter-based training offers greater flexibility in algorithmic design and optimization. In datacenter settings, centralized computation resources allow for more sophisticated noise addition, sampling strategies, and gradient aggregation techniques, which can improve both privacy and model utility [citation:txt9]. This flexibility opens the door to investigating how user-level DP can be effectively scaled to large language models, where training involves massive datasets and complex architectures with hundreds of millions of parameters [citation:txt12].

The central research question in this article is: *How can we leverage the flexibility of datacenter-based training to design and optimize user-level DP algorithms for fine-tuning large language models?* To answer this, we will explore algorithmic approaches such as example-level sampling (ELS) and user-level sampling (ULS), and examine the optimizations that make them feasible and effective in large-scale settings.
